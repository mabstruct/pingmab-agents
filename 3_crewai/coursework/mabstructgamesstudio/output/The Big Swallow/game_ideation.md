# The Big Swallow — Game Concept Briefs

*A collection of four game concept directions for the browser title **The Big Swallow**. The first is our flagship, fully-developed concept; the following three are bold alternative directions that reframe "swallow" as an act with emotional cost, keeping us clear of the saturated grow-to-eat/black-hole genre while staying true to the studio's surreal, cosmic, slightly-melancholic voice.*

---

# CONCEPT 1 — *Glutton: The Organism* — **FLAGSHIP**

## Title & Subtitle
**The Big Swallow** — *Glutton: The Organism*

## Short Idea Description
A strange, translucent alien organism awakens deep inside a derelict cosmic generation ship and discovers it is *very* hungry. Starting as a microscopic swallow-sac feeding on dust and bacteria, you eat, grow, and mutate your way up the food chain — until you're large enough to swallow the crew, the engines, and eventually the ship itself. Your final meal is the fusion core, and your final act is to ignite into a living star.

## Core Idea
*The Big Swallow* is a single-player, browser-based growth game — **Katamari meets cellular consumption** — wrapped in a surreal, melancholic sci-fi shell. Three pillars:
1. **The Joy of Escalation** — the recurring *"You can now eat X"* dopamine beat drives everything.
2. **You Are What You Eat** — consumption isn't just growth, it's transformation; keystone prey grant **mutations** that visibly reshape your silhouette and change how you play.
3. **The Empty Ship** — as you grow the world quiets; every meaningful thing you swallow leaves an **echo** (a voice/memory fragment), so by the end you are a collage of everything you've consumed — and you feel just slightly complicit.

## Key Features & Mechanics
- **Size Value (SV) system** — swallow anything with `SV < player.SV × 0.85`, keeping bigger prey tantalizingly "just out of reach"; logarithmic growth within a tier, exponential across tiers pushes players to keep hunting up.
- **7 Growth Tiers** — Motes → Vermin → Flora & Machines → Crew → Structure → The Ship → **The Core** (stellar ignition endgame), each unlocking new ship environments.
- **Mutation System (signature)** — 3 active slots; swallowing keystone prey grants situational abilities that reshape your body: *Plating* (tanky), *Corrosive* (dissolves barriers), *Stealth*, *Echo-Key* (access codes + memory), *Fireproof*, *Anchor* (zero-G immunity), *Glow*.
- **Echo System (narrative layer)** — collectible memory fragments reviewable in the "Innads" menu; ambient chatter fades as the echo-counter grows (an inverse relationship the player *feels*); late-game echoes blur and overlap.
- **Hazards, no combat, no permadeath** — fire, vacuum, UV, crushing bulkheads *strip mass* rather than kill; each hazard is a puzzle solved by eating the right mutation-granting prey.
- **Juicy feedback** — maw-stretch animations, escalating "gulp" SFX that deepens as you grow, adaptive soundtrack that layers up per tier, a triumphant sting on every threshold.
- **Session** — a single ~25–45 minute escalating arc, fully resumable; clean top-down 2D vector art.

## Why This Works as a Browser Game
The grow-to-eat loop is a browser-native staple — instantly satisfying, no tutorial, lightweight 2D/particle rendering, fast load, resumable. We elevate a hollow genre with our surreal cosmic tone; the mutation + echo layers add expressive replayability and rare emotional payoff (the growth-vs-loneliness inversion, the star-birth finale) without inflating scope. A contained ~30-minute arc with no combat, permadeath, or online infrastructure is the ideal shippable single-player browser title.

# CONCEPT 2 — *The Last Light-Eater*

## Title & Subtitle
**The Big Swallow** — *The Last Light-Eater: A Story Told in the Dark You Leave Behind*

## Short Idea Description
You are the final entity at the heat-death end of the universe: a soft, drifting mouth of shadow that survives by swallowing the last remaining lights — dying stars, distress beacons, a child's nightlight adrift in vacuum, the glow of a memory that refuses to fade. Each light keeps you alive a little longer but permanently darkens that region of the cosmos forever. You are not a monster; you are simply the one who has to turn off the lights before you go.

## Core Idea
A meditative darkness-navigation game where **light is both the only thing you can see by and the only thing you can eat.** Pillars:
1. **Illumination as Resource & Regret** — every meal shrinks your visible world; the game gets literally darker as you progress — diminishing returns you feel in your eyes, not on a bar.
2. **Each Light Is A Someone** — lights are tiny whispered stories (a lighthouse signaling a drowned coast, a probe transmitting to a dead Earth); you choose to swallow now or let it burn a little longer in the cold.
3. **The Kindness of Endings** — a game about mercy, not gluttony; some lights are suffering, and swallowing them is a release. Tone is a hospice, not a hunt.

## Key Features & Mechanics
- 2D top-down darkness navigation; your light is a small aura, swallowing refuels it but permanently dims the ambient world.
- **Swallow-or-Spare economy** — hoarding light lets you see the beauty longer, but starving means dying in the dark before the ending.
- **Constellation Memory Map** — extinguished lights leave faint after-images; the map becomes a private graveyard of all you consumed.
- **The Final Light** — the last object is *your own* glow; the final act is swallowing yourself, ending in true black and a single line of text. ~30 min arc.
- Minimal palette: black canvas, points of warm light, whisper-text.

## Why This Works as a Browser Game
Near-zero art budget — points of light on black read as intentional and gorgeous, run on any device, and screenshot beautifully for sharing. The "screen gets darker the more you eat" hook is a perfect one-line pitch, instantly understood, and the ~30-minute arc is self-limiting — ideal for a contemplative browser tab.

# CONCEPT 3 — *Swallower of Sound*

## Title & Subtitle
**The Big Swallow** — *Swallower of Sound: The Universe Is Too Loud. Someone Has to Make It Quiet.*

## Short Idea Description
The cosmos is drowning in noise — radio static, screaming pulsars, the endless hold-music of a hundred dead civilizations still broadcasting. You are a **Quiet**, a creature that swallows *sound itself*, gulping down waveforms to carve pockets of silence. But every voice you swallow, you keep — forever humming inside you — and the deeper you go, the more you realize you're eating the last evidence that anyone was ever here.

## Core Idea
An audio-first, rhythm-adjacent experience where you *see* sound as visible waveforms and *swallow* them by matching, timing, or engulfing. Pillars:
1. **Audio Is The Game** — silence is the goal and the reward; sound is the enemy, the resource, and the emotion.
2. **You Become An Archive** — every swallowed sound joins an internal chorus; late game you can "play back" your own belly — a haunting composition of things that no longer exist anywhere else.
3. **Quiet Has A Price** — the pockets of silence become peaceful, then unbearable; the game asks whether a silent universe is better than a screaming one.

## Key Features & Mechanics
- **Waveform-swallowing** — align your "mouth" to incoming sound-shapes; harmonize to swallow cleanly, mistime and the sound fractures into shrapnel you must chase.
- **The Inner Chorus** — a persistent, generative soundtrack built from your diet; every playthrough sounds different.
- **Discrete broadcasts** — small narrative sound-stories (a lullaby loop, a war's final transmission) experienced by ear before you decide to silence them.
- **Two endings** — Total Silence (swallow all) or The Great Exhale (release your chorus into a reborn, cacophonous universe). ~20–30 min.
- Procedural audio + oscilloscope-style abstract visuals.

## Why This Works as a Browser Game
The **Web Audio API is made for this** — real-time synthesis and manipulation run natively with no assets to download. Visuals are cheap, striking abstract waveforms (on-brand surreal). It's genuinely novel — there's no crowded "swallow sound" genre — and headphones-in, 25-minute contemplative sessions are a criminally underused fit for the browser.

# CONCEPT 4 — *Gulp: A Theory of Forgetting*

## Title & Subtitle
**The Big Swallow** — *Gulp: A Theory of Forgetting — You Swallowed the Universe. You Just Can't Remember Doing It.*

## Short Idea Description
You wake as a colossal, ancient mouth floating in an empty cosmos, and a soft AI voice tells you: *long ago, you swallowed everything — every star, world, and person — and stored it inside yourself.* Now you must decide, one memory at a time, what to bring back. The Big Swallow already happened. The game is the slow, aching act of *un-swallowing* — regurgitating a universe from fragments, and discovering why you ate it in the first place.

## Core Idea
A genre inversion: you don't consume the world, you **reconstruct it from what's already inside you.** Pillars:
1. **The Swallow Is In The Past** — the horror and tenderness come from re-assembling something you destroyed.
2. **Curation As Emotion** — limited "space" means you *cannot* restore everything; you choose the beautiful nebula or the small backwater planet where your guiding voice used to live.
3. **The Twist Is Empathy** — the AI helping you is a memory of a being you swallowed; you slowly learn the Swallow was an act of desperate love — you ate the universe to *save* it from something worse.

## Key Features & Mechanics
- **Regurgitation-crafting** — combine memory-fragments (a color, a name, a coordinate, a smell) to reconstitute stars, worlds, and beings on a blank cosmic canvas.
- **The Space Inside You** — a strict inventory limit forcing tragic curation; you physically cannot un-swallow it all.
- **Branching cosmos** — what you rebuild, and in what order, changes the final world-state and the AI's revelations; high replay value.
- **The final reveal** — you find your own memory last and learn what made you swallow everything; you choose the ending: keep the universe out, or swallow it one last time to protect it again. ~30–40 min.
- Papercraft / diorama-in-void 2D aesthetic — composable objects on a dark stage.

## Why This Works as a Browser Game
The core loop is **drag-drop-and-combine** — the most reliable, performant, click-friendly mechanic on the web. Assets are small composable objects on black, keeping footprint tiny. The "un-swallowing" inversion is an instant hook that separates it from every eat-to-grow game, and curation-under-scarcity delivers our signature melancholy through *player choice* rather than scripted sadness — with stop-and-start pacing perfect for a browser session.
