# The Big Swallow

*Subtitle: Feed the Void, Become the Cosmos*

---

## Short Idea Description

*The Big Swallow* is a single-player, physics-driven cosmic devouring game where you play as a newborn hungry singularity drifting through a surreal, hand-painted universe. You consume matter, bend gravity, and grow from a humble speck into a reality-warping cosmic force — but every meal is a gamble, and the universe pushes back the hungrier you get.

---

## Core Idea

You are **The Maw** — a small, sentient point of gravitational hunger born into a drifting field of asteroids, debris, and light. The Maw's only desire is to consume. Anything smaller than you can be pulled in and swallowed, adding to your mass and expanding your gravitational reach. Anything *larger* than you will tear you apart instead — spaghettifying you into strands of light before you reform, shrunken and humbled.

This isn't a twitchy `.io`-style feeding frenzy. *The Big Swallow* is a **slow-burn gravity puzzle sandbox**: momentum is preserved, mass makes you ponderous, and every swallow subtly reshapes the orbits of everything around you. The tension isn't in reflexes — it's in *judgment*: is that rock small enough to eat, or is it going to eat you?

The game's signature "wow" moment is the **stage transition**: each time you cross a mass threshold, the camera pulls back in a slow, awe-struck reveal, showing that the "universe" you were feeding in was itself just a speck inside something larger. You ascend through six **Digestion Stages** — Pebble, Rock, Planet-Eater, Star-Swallower, Galaxy-Gulper, and finally **The Big Swallow** — each one recontextualizing your sense of scale and mortality.

It's a game about appetite, scale, and the quiet horror/wonder of realizing you were always small compared to something bigger — until, maybe, you aren't anymore.

## Game Mechanics & Features

### Core Loop
1. **Drift** through a field of celestial debris using momentum-based movement.
2. **Judge** the relative size of nearby objects against your own mass.
3. **Swallow** anything smaller, using your gravity well to pull it in.
4. **Grow**, becoming slower, more powerful, and gravitationally dominant.
5. **Ascend** into a new Digestion Stage when a mass threshold is crossed, reframing the entire play field.
6. **Avoid or escape** objects larger than you, which threaten to spaghettify you.

### Movement & Physics
- **Input:** A single, simple control scheme — click-drag (or touch-drag) applies gentle directional thrust to The Maw. No twitch-steering; released input lets momentum carry you.
- **Mass-Based Inertia:** As your mass increases, thrust becomes less responsive and momentum carries further — early game feels nimble, late game feels like piloting a slow, inevitable force of nature.
- **Gravity Well:** Your gravitational radius is visualized as a soft distortion ring around The Maw. Objects within it begin to curve toward you; the closer and smaller, the stronger the pull.
- **Orbital Chain Reactions:** Nudging one body can cause it to collide with others, cracking large objects into smaller, swallowable fragments — rewarding players who "shepherd" the field rather than chase individual targets.

### Growth & Threat System
- **Mass Comparison Rule:** Every object has a visible relative-mass indicator (a subtle glow/size cue). If an object's mass is below your threshold, it's swallowable; if above, it's lethal on contact.
- **Risk/Reward Scaling:** The closer an object's mass is to your own (without exceeding it), the greater the growth bonus for eating it — encouraging tense, deliberate near-miss gambles rather than only eating "safe" small fry.
- **Spaghettification:** Colliding with a too-large object triggers a slow-motion stretch-and-shatter animation; The Maw is torn into strands of light and reforms at a reduced mass, dropping back a partial stage. Death is a setback, not a hard fail-state, preserving flow.
- **Anti-Mass Hazards:** Glowing "anti-mass motes" shrink you if swallowed — visually tempting (bright, easy to reach) but punishing, teaching players to read intent, not just proximity.
- **Environmental Hazards:**
  - *Pulsars* — emit rhythmic shockwave pulses that push The Maw off its trajectory, demanding route replanning.
  - *Dark-Matter Clouds* — obscure vision and dampen gravity pull within their radius.
  - *Rogue AI Probes* — small, erratic-moving hazards that actively dodge or flee, adding a light chase dynamic without breaking the game's slow pace.

### The Six Digestion Stages
Each stage introduces new object types, new hazards, new visual palettes, and a distinct "feel" of scale:

1. **Pebble** — swallow dust, gravel, and stray asteroid chips in a tight asteroid field.
2. **Rock** — consume moons, comets, and satellite debris; first pulsar hazards appear.
3. **Planet-Eater** — swallow whole planets and their orbiting rings; gravity begins visibly bending starlight.
4. **Star-Swallower** — consume dying stars, absorb solar flares; dark-matter clouds appear.
5. **Galaxy-Gulper** — swallow star clusters and rogue AI probe-ships; space itself begins to visibly warp and ripple.
6. **The Big Swallow** — the final, reality-bending tier where you consume galaxies, nebulae, and — implied in the closing sequence — the boundary of the universe itself.

### Stage Transition — "Collapse-and-Bloom"
- Triggered on crossing a mass milestone.
- The play field freezes, then **zooms out** in a slow, deliberate reveal: what looked like the "whole universe" a moment ago is shown to be a small pocket inside a vastly larger structure.
- A shimmering ascension chime plays; UI briefly clears to let the reveal breathe.
- This moment repeats at each of the five transitions, escalating in scale and visual grandeur, culminating in the final Big Swallow reveal.

### Between-Stage Interlude — "The Belly of the Void"
- A short, non-interactive (or lightly interactive) interstitial screen between major stages.
- Shows a swirling, dreamlike collage of everything consumed so far — asteroids, moons, stars — drifting inside The Maw's impossible interior.
- Functions as a narrative beat, a visual palate-cleanser, and a soft loading screen.

### Game Modes
- **Campaign / Score Mode:** A timed run through all six stages, scored on total mass consumed, near-miss swallows, and time-to-ascend. Suited to quick 3–5 minute sessions.
- **Zen / Free-Feed Mode:** No timer, no fail-state pressure beyond spaghettification setbacks — an endless, meditative sandbox for players who want to simply drift and grow.

### Progression & Meta
- **Swallowed-Matter Currency:** Mass consumed converts into a persistent currency between runs.
- **Maw Skins (Cosmetic):** Ink Void, Prism Rift, Starving Nebula, and other visual reskins of The Maw's core appearance.
- **Passive Perks:** Wider gravity radius, slower spaghettification stretch (more reaction time to escape), faster mass-recovery after a shrink event.
- **No pay-to-win systems** — all unlocks are earned through play, keeping the single-player, meditative tone intact.

### UI & Feedback
- Minimal on-screen UI: a subtle mass meter, current stage indicator, and gravity-radius ring are the only persistent HUD elements.
- Relative-size cues are read primarily through visual scale and glow, not numbers — preserving the "feel" over "stat-check" experience.
- Screenshot/clip-friendly stage transitions are treated as a deliberate shareability feature.

## Art & Design Direction

### Visual Identity
- **Hand-painted, surreal cosmic aesthetic** — soft nebula gradients, painterly starfields, and organic, non-geometric shapes rather than clean sci-fi hardware. The universe should feel *alive and dreamed*, not engineered.
- **The Maw's design** evolves across stages — beginning as a small flickering point of dark light with a faint corona, gradually gaining more visible "presence": tendrils of gravitational distortion, a deepening void-black core, and a subtly expressive, almost creature-like silhouette by the Galaxy-Gulper stage.
- **Color Language by Stage:**
  - *Pebble/Rock:* warm dusty browns, soft asteroid greys, muted starlight.
  - *Planet-Eater:* richer blues and greens, planetary bands and ring textures.
  - *Star-Swallower:* hot oranges, whites, and solar flare golds.
  - *Galaxy-Gulper:* deep purples, star-cluster sparkle, warping light trails.
  - *The Big Swallow:* an otherworldly palette beyond the previous stages' logic — shifting iridescence, impossible colors implying reality breaking down.

### Object Design
- Every swallowable object is rendered with clear silhouette read at a glance (round asteroids, ringed planets, flare-edged stars) so relative size/danger is instantly legible even before UI cues.
- Anti-mass motes and hazards use a distinct visual "wrongness" — subtle glitch shimmer, inverted glow, or unnatural stillness — to reward players who learn to read subtle tells over time.

### Motion & Animation
- Movement throughout is **slow, weighty, and fluid** — no sharp or twitchy animation. Even danger (pulsar shockwaves, hazard warnings) telegraphs with graceful, readable buildup rather than jump-scare suddenness.
- The **swallow animation** — a soft pull-in, brief compression, and gentle pulse-out — is the game's key tactile "feel" moment and should be endlessly satisfying to repeat.
- **Spaghettification** is rendered as a slow-motion, almost balletic stretch into light-strands before a snap-to-black and quiet reform — treating "death" as visually beautiful rather than punishing.
- **Stage transitions** use long, slow zoom-outs (2–4 seconds) with soft camera easing, giving the reveal room to land emotionally.

### Audio Direction
- **Ambient evolving drone score** — layers of harmonic texture are added at each Digestion Stage, so growth is *heard* as much as seen.
- **Swallow SFX** — a wet, resonant "whumph" with sub-bass thud, pitch-shifting deeper as The Maw grows, reinforcing scale through sound.
- **Gravitational hum** — a low-frequency ambient pulse tied to gravity-radius size, subtly intensifying when danger (an oversized object) enters range.
- **Spaghettification sting** — a stretched, warping glissando snapping into a hollow beat of silence before quiet reform.
- **Ascension chime** — a shimmering, awe-inspiring chord swell timed to each stage transition's zoom-out.
- **Spatial audio cues** — objects entering the gravity well emit soft, panned tones so players can *hear* their next meal approaching, supporting the meditative, low-visual-clutter HUD philosophy.
