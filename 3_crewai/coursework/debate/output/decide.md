The **opposition is more convincing**. The proposition reasonably limits access through warrants, necessity, time limits, and independent oversight, but it does not adequately answer the opposition’s central argument: creating exceptional access to encrypted communications may introduce systemic vulnerabilities that courts and legal safeguards cannot technically contain.

The opposition also explains that such powers risk mission creep, chill lawful speech, expose sensitive information, and may be ineffective because sophisticated criminals can switch tools or methods. Crucially, it offers less intrusive investigative alternatives rather than treating privacy as immunity from investigation. On balance, the proposition establishes a legitimate public-safety objective, but the opposition provides the stronger account of why the proposed power could create broader dangers without reliably preventing crime.
